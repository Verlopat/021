\section{Model and Definitions}\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{System.} Processes $P=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$; at most $f$ are Byzantine. Computation proceeds in synchronous communication-closed rounds. Honest-to-honest messages are delivered; Byzantine processes may equivocate, omit, and choose adaptively after observing honest broadcasts.
\paragraph{Authentication.} Model B is the default: direct channels identify the sender but nothing is transferable. Model A adds unforgeable signatures. All resilience claims in this paper refer to Model B.
\paragraph{Observations.} For a multiset $M$ of messages $(s,\kappa,v,\mu)$,
\[
A_0^{\mathrm{blind}}(M)=\{\!\{\kappa\}\!\},\quad
A_0(M)=\{\!\{(\kappa,v)\}\!\},\quad
A_1(M)=\left(A_0(M),\{(s,\kappa,v)\}\right),\quad
A_2(M)=M.
\]
The induced equivalence is $M_1\sim_i M_2\iff A_i(M_1)=A_i(M_2)$.
\paragraph{Algorithms.} A level-$L_i$ round transition factors through $A_i$: $T(s,M)=\widehat T(s,A_i(M))$. The implementation enforces this by passing only abstraction observations to protocols.
\paragraph{Problems.} Crusader agreement, connected consensus on the spider graph, multivalued Byzantine agreement, and $\varepsilon$-approximate agreement with convex-hull validity.

\section{Theorems and Research Obligations}

\begin{theorem}[L0 factorization consequence]
If $T$ factors through $A_0$ and $A_0(M_1)=A_0(M_2)$, then
$T(s,M_1)=T(s,M_2)$.
\end{theorem}

This theorem and the equivalence laws of $\sim_0$ are machine-checked in Lean.

\subsection{Separation obligation}

For a selected problem $\Pi$, an L0/L1 separation requires both:

\[
\Pi\in\operatorname{Solv}(L_1,n>3f)
\]

and

\[
\Pi\notin\operatorname{Solv}(L_0,n>3f).
\]

The software searches for finite indistinguishability witnesses and protocol counterexamples. A finite counterexample is evidence for a specific protocol/model instance, not by itself a universal impossibility theorem.

Similarly, an L1/L2 separation requires a problem for which L2 solves the specification while L1 cannot under the same formal assumptions.

\subsection{Resilience characterization}

The target matrix contains Crusader Agreement, Connected Consensus, Multidimensional Approximate Agreement, Multivalued Byzantine Agreement, Set Agreement, and Vector Agreement. Every cell must ultimately be supported by a construction, impossibility proof, or exactly matched literature theorem.

Until that support exists, the cell remains a research result rather than a theorem claim.
